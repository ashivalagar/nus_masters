# Make It Stick: how to actually remember this

Five habits, in order of payoff. Apply them to every concept note.

1. **Predict, then check.** Before running a Visual Lab or reading a solution, commit to an answer on paper. The wrong guess is the lesson. See [[Visual Labs]].
2. **Teach it to a 12-year-old (Feynman).** Close the note, explain the concept aloud in two minutes using the analogy from its *Mental picture*. Where you stall is what to re-read.
3. **Blank-page recall.** Next day, write what you remember of yesterday's topic, then compare. Retrieval beats re-reading.
4. **Space it.** Weekends only, so revisit: same-weekend (end of day), next weekend (10 mins), two weeks later. The [[Weekend Study Plan]] builds this in.
5. **Do a past-paper-style problem within 24 h** of learning a topic: [[EE5101 Past Paper Analysis]] and [[EE5907 Exam Style and Proxy Papers]].

## One-line analogy cheat sheet
- Eigenvector: direction a matrix only stretches, never turns.
- Pole: where the system's personality lives; left half = calm, right = explosion.
- Controllability: can your knob reach every room?  Observability: can your windows see every room?
- Pole placement: choose the personality; cost is effort.
- LQR: budget dial between error and effort.
- Integrator: cruise control remembering the hill.
- Observer: dead-reckoning corrected by GPS fixes.
- Bayes: a crowd of 1000 people; count the red dots among positives.
- MLE vs MAP: data alone vs data plus a sensible hunch.
- ROC: smoke-alarm sensitivity dial.
- Perceptron: nudge the fence when a sheep is on the wrong side.
- MLP: bend the space until a line separates.
- PCA: best shadow of the data; LDA: best shadow for separating classes.
- K-means: tutors move to the centre of their students.
- SVM: widest street; support vectors are the kerb posts.
- Gradient descent: hiker in fog; learning rate is stride length.
